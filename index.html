The grove

One olive
in the moonlight

Olives ripen on the tree through the autumn, turning from green to violet. The earlier they are picked, the greener and more peppery their oil.

The harvest

Picked after dark

The olives are picked by hand in the cool of the night, so the fruit stays firm and fresh on its way to the press.

The next stop is the press.

The press

Inside the olive

Every olive has three layers: a thin skin, soft flesh and a hard stone. Almost all of the oil sits in the flesh.

SkinA thin, waxy skin protects the fruit while it ripens on the tree.

FleshThe soft flesh holds the oil in tiny droplets inside its cells.

StoneThe stone is crushed with the fruit, then separated from the oil with the rest of the paste.

The oil

A taste in three notes

Olive oil is tasted like wine: first its aroma, then its bitterness, then the peppery catch at the back of the throat.

The collection

Three expressions

Three oils for three uses, from a green early harvest to a mild oil for the kitchen.

Extra virgin, early harvest

Nocturne

Green and grassy, pressed from the first olives of the season.

Virgin, golden

Reserva

Golden and rounded, from riper olives. An oil for everyday cooking.

Light, mild

Primera

Light in taste and colour, and steady at high heat for baking and frying.
